01 · Objective

Research question and dataset

This study uses the STEW dataset (Lim, Sourina & Wang, 2018): 48 participants recorded with a 14-channel Emotiv EPOC headset in two 2.5-minute sessions, a resting baseline and the SIMKAP multitasking test, which combines visual search with arithmetic and memory questions. Participants rated their workload on a 1–9 scale after each session. Although the files are labelled lo and hi, the dataset documentation identifies lo as the resting recording rather than a low-difficulty task.

All 45 participants who provided ratings rated the test above rest (median 1 at rest, 7 during the test). The classification target is therefore rest versus test. All models are evaluated on unseen participants: the 48 participants are split into 8 groups of 6, and each group is predicted by a model trained on the remaining 42.

02 · Signals

Raw and filtered EEG

Seconds 60–70 of eight recordings, before and after filtering: six from the three participants with the fewest rejected windows, two from the participant with the most.

Spectrum of the first selected channel

Welch power spectral density over the whole recording, raw and filtered, log scale.

Observations

The raw signal carries a constant offset of several thousand units (removed for display) and slow drift. Large low-frequency deflections on AF3, AF4, F7 and F8 mostly reflect blinks and eye movements; short high-frequency bursts on T7 and T8 typically reflect jaw or temporal muscle activity.

All raw spectra fall off steeply above approximately 42 Hz due to the headset's internal filtering, so the analysis is restricted to 1–45 Hz.

05 · Artifacts

During the test, participants move their eyes across the screen and may tense facial and jaw muscles. These sources produce signals much larger than cortical activity, and the eye-adjacent electrodes are among the most important features. 06 · Findings

Key findings

The full feature set outperforms the classic θ/α workload index

The frontal-θ / parietal-α ratio, a standard EEG workload index, achieves a window AUC of 0.645 on held-out people and classifies 65.6% of recordings correctly. Logistic regression on all 282 features achieves 0.881 and 86.5%.

Model choice has no significant effect

Logistic regression, SVM, random forest and gradient boosting achieve between 0.863 and 0.881 AUC. Paired per-participant tests against logistic regression give Holm-corrected p = 1.00 (SVM (RBF kernel)); p = 1.00 (Random forest); p = 1.00 (Gradient boosting). Additional model flexibility yields no gain; performance is limited by inter-individual variability: participant identity explains a median 37% of a feature's variance, rest vs. test 0.8%.

Right frontal and temporal channels contribute most

Permuting one channel's features at a time, the largest AUC drops occur for F8, right frontal, beside the eye (0.165); T8, right temple (0.047); O2, right occipital (0.039). By feature type, relative θ power matters most (AUC drop 0.084). Section 05 examines the extent to which this reflects ocular and muscle activity.

Ocular artifacts explain part, but not all, of the performance

Six artifact markers alone achieve 0.765 (95% CI 0.71–0.82). The horizontal eye-movement proxy (F7 − F8, 1–4 Hz) is the strongest single marker, with AUC 0.74; blink rate does not differ between conditions (median 3.8 vs 4.4 per minute, p = 0.51). Removing eye components with ICA gives 0.827 (95% CI 0.79–0.87), and using only the four posterior channels gives 0.817 (95% CI 0.77–0.86): 0.054–0.064 below the full model, but well above chance. Removing γ, where jaw and forehead muscle activity dominates, has no measurable effect (Δ 0.000, 95% CI -0.010 to +0.010).

Self-reported workload could not be predicted from EEG

Within the test condition, ridge regression on EEG features did not outperform a mean-only baseline in predicting 1–9 ratings: R² = -0.05 (mean-only baseline -0.05), MAE 1.25, n = 45. The classifier output was also uncorrelated with the ratings (Spearman ρ = 0.17, p = 0.28). The EEG distinguishes the two conditions but shows no evidence of tracking perceived task difficulty. With n = 45, this test has limited power and does not rule out a relationship.

Within-participant accuracy is inflated by recording drift

Trained on the first half of each participant's recordings and tested on the second half, the model achieves 0.985. However, a model distinguishing early from late windows within a single recording, where the condition is constant, also performs above chance (median AUC 0.63 over 86 recordings; 20 of them ≥ 0.8), indicating that recording drift makes segments partly identifiable. Across people, training on first halves and testing on second halves leaves AUC unchanged (0.872 vs 0.874). Per-participant normalisation did not improve performance: median per-person AUC 0.97 without it, 0.93 with a z-score, 0.97 with a resting baseline.

07 · Limitations

Limitations

  • Rest is not a low-workload condition. The two recordings differ in visual input, eye movement, posture and motor activity as well as mental effort, so the classifier separates rest from task engagement rather than levels of workload. Estimating workload level would require a dataset with graded task difficulty.
  • Ocular and muscle signals cannot be fully separated from neural activity. STEW has no EOG or EMG channels. The artifact checks above remove what can be identified from the EEG itself; they cannot prove the remainder is neural.
  • One session, one recording per condition. Each condition is a single continuous 2.5-minute recording made on one day. Models calibrated on part of a recording and tested on the remainder can recognise the recording itself (electrode contact, headset position, drift), so within-person results are an upper bound. Nothing here tests stability across days.
  • Small effective sample. Windows from the same recording are correlated; the independent unit is the participant (n = 48), which is why confidence intervals resample participants.
  • Unknown units and reference. The dataset documentation specifies neither; amplitude thresholds assume approximately µV units.
  • Offline processing. Zero-phase filtering, whole-recording bad-channel detection and per-recording ICA need the full recording. A live system would need causal equivalents.
08 · Methods

Methods

Code, notebooks and tests are available in the repository.

Filtering

A Butterworth filter of order \(n\) has a flat pass-band, \(|H(f)|^2 = 1/\big(1+(f/f_c)^{2n}\big)\). Running it forwards and then backwards (sosfiltfilt) gives \(H(f)\,H^*(f)=|H(f)|^2\): real-valued, so there is no phase shift, and the roll-off doubles. Second-order sections keep the 1 Hz high-pass (1/64 of Nyquist) numerically stable. The 50 Hz notch (\(Q=30\), about 1.7 Hz wide) places zeros on the unit circle at \(e^{\pm j2\pi f_0/f_s}\).

Artifact statistics

Outliers are measured with the median absolute deviation, \(\hat\sigma = 1.4826\cdot\mathrm{median}|x-\mathrm{median}(x)|\), which, unlike the standard deviation, is not inflated by the outliers themselves. A window is dropped if any good channel exceeds ±200 after filtering or the window overlaps a saturated or flat segment.

Spectral features

Each 4 s window is split into \(K=5\) Hann-tapered 2 s segments with 75% overlap: \[\hat P(f)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{f_s\sum_n w[n]^2}\Big|\sum_{n}w[n]\,x_k[n]\,e^{-j2\pi fn/f_s}\Big|^2\] Averaging \(K\) segments cuts the periodogram's variance roughly \(K\)-fold. Band power is \(P_b=\sum_{f\in[f_1,f_2)}\hat P(f)\,\Delta f\) with \(\Delta f = 0.5\) Hz; relative power is \(P_b/P_{1\text{–}45}\). Bands: δ 1–4, θ 4–8, α 8–13, β 13–30, γ 30–45 Hz. Individual alpha peaks at rest ranged 7.5–12.5 Hz in the 38 participants where one was detectable.

Spectral entropy is \(H=-\sum_k p_k\log p_k/\log N\) with \(p_k=\hat P(f_k)/\sum_j \hat P(f_j)\). Ratios (log10): θ/α, θ/β, β/(α+θ) (Pope et al., 1995), and frontal θ over parietal α. Hjorth mobility is \(\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(x')/\mathrm{var}(x)}\); complexity is mobility(\(x'\)) / mobility(\(x\)).

Models and validation

Logistic regression with an L2 penalty, \(P(y=1\mid x)=\sigma(w^\top x+b)\), minimising log-loss \(+\tfrac{1}{2C}\lVert w\rVert^2\); an RBF-kernel SVM; a 400-tree random forest; histogram gradient boosting. Outer GroupKFold(8) over participants, inner GroupKFold(4) over the training participants to choose hyper-parameters. Median imputation of excluded channels and standardisation are fitted inside each training fold. Random seed 0 throughout (folds, forests, boosting, bootstrap, ICA).

ROC-AUC is the probability that a randomly chosen test-condition window scores higher than a randomly chosen rest window.

Ocular correction (ICA)

Per recording, FastICA with one component per good channel is fitted on the filtered signal. Two eye proxies are built from the EEG: VEOG = mean(AF3, AF4) and HEOG = F7 − F8, both low-passed at 4 Hz. For each proxy the best-matching component is removed if its low-passed time course correlates with the proxy at |r| ≥ 0.7, so at most two components per recording. Because the proxies are frontal EEG, a removed component can also contain frontal brain activity.
