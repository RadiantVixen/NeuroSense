01 · About

Can EEG tell rest from mental multitasking — in a new person?

Mental workload monitoring could support pilots, operators and learners, but EEG varies enormously between people. NeuroSense asks a deliberately strict question: trained on some people, can a model detect task-induced workload in someone it has never seen?

Objective

Build a transparent, end-to-end EEG pipeline — preprocessing, feature engineering, classical machine learning — and evaluate it with participant-independent cross-validation so reported numbers reflect generalisation to new users.

Research motivation

Most published STEW results mix windows from the same person into training and test sets, which inflates accuracy. Here every evaluation is on held-out participants, artifacts are treated as a potential confound, and limitations are reported alongside results.

What the labels mean. Per the dataset documentation, lo files are resting-state recordings and hi files are recorded during the SIMKAP multitasking test. The task is therefore rest vs. task-induced workload, not low- vs. high-difficulty task. All 45 rated participants reported higher workload during the test (median 1 at rest vs 7 during the test, on a 1–9 scale).

What you are looking at

  • Raw signals carry a ~4 300-unit DC offset (removed for display) and slow drift.
  • Filtered signals: 50 Hz notch + zero-phase 1–45 Hz Butterworth band-pass.
  • Large slow deflections on AF3/AF4/F7/F8 are typically eye blinks or movements; sharp bursts on T7/T8 are often jaw/temporal muscle.
  • The steep drop above ~42 Hz in every raw spectrum is the headset's on-board filter — the reason the analysis stops at 45 Hz.
  • The last recording in the list is the participant with the most rejected windows, shown for honesty.

06 · Technical Documentation

Methods & mathematics

The complete code, notebooks and tests are in the repository. The key operations, in brief:

Zero-phase Butterworth filtering

A Butterworth filter of order \(n\) has maximally flat pass-band magnitude \(|H(f)|^2 = 1/\big(1+(f/f_c)^{2n}\big)\). Running it forward and then backward (sosfiltfilt) gives the overall response \(H(f)\,H^*(f)=|H(f)|^2\): real-valued, hence zero phase (no time shift), with doubled roll-off. Second-order sections keep the 1 Hz high-pass (1/64 of Nyquist) numerically stable. A notch at 50 Hz (\(Q=30\), bandwidth ≈ 1.7 Hz) places zeros on the unit circle at \(e^{\pm j2\pi f_0/f_s}\).

Robust artifact statistics

Amplitude outliers are measured with the median absolute deviation, \(\hat\sigma = 1.4826\cdot\mathrm{median}|x-\mathrm{median}(x)|\), which, unlike the standard deviation, is not inflated by the artifacts it is meant to detect. Windows are rejected if any good channel exceeds ±200 (≈ µV) after filtering, or overlaps a saturated / flat-lined segment.

Welch power spectral density & band power

Each 4 s window is split into \(K=5\) Hann-tapered 2 s segments with 75% overlap: \[\hat P(f)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{f_s\sum_n w[n]^2}\Big|\sum_{n}w[n]\,x_k[n]\,e^{-j2\pi fn/f_s}\Big|^2\] Averaging reduces the variance of the periodogram (whose standard deviation equals its mean) roughly by \(K\). Band power is \(P_b=\sum_{f\in[f_1,f_2)}\hat P(f)\,\Delta f\) with \(\Delta f = 0.5\) Hz; by Parseval the total equals the signal variance. Relative power is \(P_b/P_{1\text{–}45}\).

Bands: δ 1–4, θ 4–8, α 8–13, β 13–30, γ 30–45 Hz. The upper limit follows the headset's on-board roll-off; individual alpha peaks of the participants ranged 7.5–12.5 Hz.

Spectral entropy, ratios, Hjorth parameters

Spectral entropy: \(H=-\sum_k p_k\log p_k/\log N\) with \(p_k=\hat P(f_k)/\sum_j \hat P(f_j)\) — 0 for a pure tone, 1 for white noise.

Ratios (log10): θ/α, θ/β, engagement index β/(α+θ) (Pope et al., 1995), and frontal θ / parietal α (task-load index).

Hjorth: activity \(\mathrm{var}(x)\); mobility \(\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(x')/\mathrm{var}(x)}\) (≈ mean frequency); complexity = mobility(\(x'\)) / mobility(\(x\)).

Models & validation

Logistic regression: \(P(y=1\mid x)=\sigma(w^\top x+b)\), minimising log-loss \(+\tfrac{1}{2C}\lVert w\rVert^2\). SVM: maximum-margin classifier with RBF kernel \(k(x,x')=e^{-\gamma\lVert x-x'\rVert^2}\). Random forest: bagged, feature-subsampled trees. Gradient boosting: trees fitted sequentially to the negative gradient of the log-loss.

Outer GroupKFold(8) over participants; inner GroupKFold(4) over training participants for hyper-parameters; imputation and standardisation fitted inside each training fold. AUC is threshold-free: the probability that a random test window scores above a random rest window.
